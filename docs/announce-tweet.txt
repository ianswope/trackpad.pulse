Every trackpad settings panel asks you to guess. How fast do your fingers actually move? Is the acceleration curve's Start anywhere your hand ever goes? So I built Trackpad Pulse for Omarchy: a plugin that records what your fingers do and fits the curve to them.

It starts with a fact I had to dig out of libinput's headers: the x axis of a custom acceleration curve is device speed in units per millisecond, and touchpads are normalized to 1000 dpi, so one unit is 25.4 mm/s. That makes it a physical axis. So the recorder keeps a time-weighted histogram of your finger speed and draws it under the curve you are editing. Then a button, Optimize for my hand, reads a week of your touches and proposes: Start where 45% of your movement is slower, End at the 90th percentile, and gains nudged by at most 10% a pass from two fingerprints a wrong curve leaves behind. An overshoot correction is a long move answered at once by a short move back the other way: the cursor went too far at that speed. A re-stroke is a long move continued at once in the same direction: the pad ran out before the cursor arrived. Every proposed change comes with its reason and the numbers. Nothing changes until you press Apply, each pass is logged, and the next one tells you whether the last one helped. And it keeps watching: every ten minutes it re-runs the check against the settings you actually have, and lights the button when your hand has changed its mind. Tonight mine said half of my movement is under 20 mm/s and 72% of it sits in the precision zone.

The bar icon is the pad itself. It lights where your fingers are, trails as they move, ripples on a tap, dims when you switch the pad off. Behind it: touches, taps, clicks, pointer moves, two-finger scrolls, pinches, three- and four-finger swipes and palms the firmware rejected (112 today, apparently I rest my thumb), distance and speed on every clock from the trailing minute to all time, a heatmap of where you touch, touches per hour of the day, a week of history recorded while the panel is closed.

Gestures got a page. Every three- and four-finger swipe and pinch Hyprland offers, each with a searchable dropdown of 137 actions on my box: slide between workspaces 1:1, fullscreen, close, float, scratchpad, next and previous and random theme, next background, the Omarchy menu, screenshots, lock, volume, zoom with a pinch, and the default terminal or any installed app opened as a fullscreen window. Pick one and it is live, because the plugin writes one Lua file of hl.gesture lines into Omarchy's toggles and asks Hyprland to reload. Four fingers right is my next theme now.

A Report page reads which hand you use from where your thumb rests and where palms fall, measures mouse against trackpad by active time, and offers an opt-in auto-off: fifteen seconds of mouse use switches the pad off, and a tap or a real move on the pad switches it straight back on, because the kernel keeps reporting the pad while Hyprland ignores it. Every touch is stamped with the window it landed in, so the week's top apps are on the same page.

The access model respects Omarchy's decision to keep users out of the input group. The recorder probes; if it cannot read the pad it falls back to cursor-only and offers a udev rule scoped to touchpads through one polkit prompt, removable from the same page. Nothing leaves the machine. No page scrolls.

All of this is built on Trackpad Plus by @davidfano, kept whole: his per-device controls, his libinput-validated curve editor, his journalled state files and his test suite, which still passes. He built it on Andrew Kent's omarchy-touchpad-widget. MIT, with both their names on it.
